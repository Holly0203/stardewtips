A full silo does not feed animals in a basic or Big building by itself. Take hay from the hopper and put it on the bench. Deluxe buildings handle that step automatically, provided there is hay in storage.

> **At a glance**
>
> - A silo holds **240 Hay** and can be built before you own animals.
> - Stored hay supplies the building’s hopper; it does not automatically mean the feeding bench is filled.
> - **Deluxe** Coops and Barns have autofeeders. Basic and Big buildings need manual feeding.
> - Use **animals × 28** as a deliberately cautious Winter stock target, rather than an exact consumption forecast.
> - In 1.6, outdoor grass survives Winter but becomes dormant and yields less hay when cut.

## Build storage before clearing your grazing patch

Robin builds a silo for **100g, 100 Stone, 10 Clay, and 5 Copper Bars**. It occupies a **3 × 3** footprint and takes **two days** to build. Its storage works with coops and barns regardless of distance, so you do not need to spend prime grazing space beside the animal door. These details are documented in the [Silo reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Silo).

Choose a location that is out of the way but easy enough to inspect. The best place is usually the one that avoids blocking a route you already use. A tidy line of buildings is less valuable if it forces a long detour every morning.

If animals are on your near-term plan, preserve a useful patch of grass until the silo is ready. Clearing the entire farm first feels productive, but removes an opportunity to collect feed while making space. For broader building placement, see our [farm layout guide](/guides/farm-layout-tips/).

## Understand what harvesting hay actually does

Once there is free silo capacity, cutting farm grass with a scythe can add hay directly to storage. A basic scythe does not guarantee hay from every cleared tile. If storage is already full, cutting more grass will not keep adding a hidden surplus. The [Hay entry](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Hay) describes the harvesting conditions.

That gives you two checks before a large clearing session: is the silo finished, and is there room inside? If either answer is no, decide whether clearing is urgent enough to give up the potential feed. You may still need a path or building footprint; the point is to make that trade deliberately.

Keep a grazing patch separate from the area you plan to clear for storage. Outdoor feeding and stored feed serve different situations, so stripping every remaining patch just because your silo has room can create more work later. A small reserve is easier to manage when you can see which area is meant for animals and which is meant for harvesting.

## Follow hay from silo to feeding bench

Think of the system as three stages:

1. **Storage:** the farm’s silos hold the shared supply.
2. **Access:** the hopper inside an animal building lets you withdraw it.
3. **Feeding:** hay must reach the building’s feeding bench.

In a manually fed building, take hay from the hopper and place it on the bench. In a Deluxe building, the autofeeder handles filling the bench from available storage. A Big building is an intermediate upgrade, so do not assume “upgraded” means automatic. The [feeding section of the Hay reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Hay) distinguishes these behaviors.

When something looks wrong, inspect the bench before buying more animals or rebuilding your silo. A full supply does not solve a missed feeding step. Equally, an autofeeder cannot replace hay that has run out.

Make the check part of an existing route: visit the animals, look at the bench, then check your reserve when it is getting low. A routine attached to another daily task is easier to remember than a separate emergency chore.

## Calculate a Winter reserve with breathing room

For a simple planning target, allow **one hay per animal for each of Winter’s 28 days**. This intentionally overestimates some in-game consumption because there are festival exceptions. It is a safety budget, not a claim that every animal eats on every calendar day.

Here is the arithmetic using a 240-unit silo:

| Animals to budget for | Conservative 28-day reserve | Silos if all feed stays in silos |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 112 Hay | 1 |
| 8 | 224 Hay | 1 |
| 12 | 336 Hay | 2 |
| 16 | 448 Hay | 2 |
| 24 | 672 Hay | 3 |

The calculation is **animal count × 28**, then round the reserve divided by 240 upward for storage. If you expect to buy or hatch more animals, include those in the target rather than budgeting only for today’s residents.

You can keep inventory hay in a chest and reload it into silo storage when needed. That makes an additional silo a convenience choice rather than the only solution. However, chest reserves need your attention: an autofeeder draws from silo storage, not whichever chest happens to be nearby.

## Do not rely on Winter grass as the main plan

Older advice often says grass disappears at the start of Winter. For 1.6, that is outdated: grass persists outdoors but stops growing and produces less hay when harvested. Animals also cannot simply graze outside through Winter. The [Grass reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Grass) and [Silo version history](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Silo) explain the change.

Treat surviving grass as a limited reserve, not a renewable Winter supply. Plan the main stock while growth is still active, then leave yourself time to fix a shortage before the season turns. Our [Winter guide](/guides/winter-guide/) covers the other projects you can fit around animal care.

## What if your reserve is already too small?

First count the animals you have, then check both silo and chest supplies. Distinguish “not enough for the season” from “not enough on today’s bench”; those require different immediate actions.

Marnie sells hay for **50g each**, according to the [Hay reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Hay). As a planning example, buying a missing 100 Hay costs 5,000g. Compare that bill with your available money before making another livestock purchase. Saving enough for the animal but nothing for its food is a sign to pause the expansion.

If you have feed in a chest, move it into the shared supply and check the bench. If you need to buy feed, do not postpone the trip until your last piece has gone. Shop access and your own schedule can make a just-in-time plan surprisingly inconvenient.

## A small end-of-Fall checklist

Count current and expected animals, set a reserve target, inspect storage capacity, and confirm which buildings require manual feeding. Then keep a little spending money available for corrections. You do not need a perfect farm design to maintain a dependable food supply.
