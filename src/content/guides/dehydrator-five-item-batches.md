Four matching fruit are not enough, and three normal-quality fruit plus two gold-quality copies do not make a valid batch. A Dehydrator needs **five of the same type and quality**. Sorting that one requirement often solves a machine that appears unwilling to load.

## Find out whether you already own one

The mushroom cave includes a Dehydrator. Otherwise, Pierre sells its crafting recipe for **10,000g**, and each machine uses **30 Wood, 2 Clay, and 1 Fire Quartz**. There are also reward routes, so inspect the equipment you have before paying for a recipe solely to obtain a first machine.

The [Dehydrator entry](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Dehydrator) documents acquisition and accepted batches. Choosing the [farm cave](/guides/farm-cave-mushrooms-or-bats-guide/) is a permanent normal-play decision; the included machine is a consideration, not a reason to ignore the cave outputs you prefer.

## The five-item rule changes the storage problem

Use separate stacks for quality levels. If the harvest gives eight normal-quality fruit and two silver ones, you have one normal batch and leftovers, not two complete batches.

Before moving food into the machine chest, keep anything you need for gifts, bundles, or an immediate recipe. A stack of five may look convenient to process while also containing the only copies reserved for another use.

For a small harvest, leftovers may accumulate slowly. There is no requirement to keep every unmatched item until it reaches five. Selling a few copies can be reasonable when the alternative is a chest full of tiny stacks you rarely revisit.

## Compare the batch with all five raw items

Ordinary Dried Fruit and Dried Mushrooms sell for **7.5 × the input's base price + 25g** per batch before profession bonuses. The finished product has normal quality. Grapes instead produce Raisins; Red Mushrooms are not accepted.

For hypothetical normal-quality fruit with a **100g base price**:

| Choice | Value for five fruit |
|---|---:|
| Sell five raw | 500g |
| One ordinary dried batch | 775g |
| Value added by drying | 275g |

The 775g is the price of the entire batch, not each fruit. Dividing by five gives an effective **155g per input** in this example.

Higher-quality raw inputs could have sold for more while the dried output stays the same. Recalculate the opportunity cost before loading your best-quality stack. The machine's ability to accept it does not establish that it is the most valuable use.

## Why use it instead of a jar or keg?

A batch is ready the following morning. That makes the machine useful when a large stockpile needs moving through limited processing space, even if another method adds more value to each individual fruit.

Imagine you have fifty fruit and only a few spare machines. You are choosing between the maximum return per fruit and the amount of backlog you can clear while new harvests keep arriving. Our [keg and jar comparison](/guides/keg-vs-preserves-jar/) explains that distinction for single-input machines.

If the harvest is small and your kegs have idle space, there may be less reason to dry it. If you have several chests of berries, faster five-item batches deserve a closer look. Neither situation requires declaring one machine universally better.

## Check the item rather than its appearance

The machine accepts suitable fruit and mushrooms, not every edible-looking forage item. Truffles are not treated as mushrooms for this purpose. If loading fails, check item type, count, and quality before crafting another machine.

## A normal Blueberry batch

Five normal Blueberries could sell raw for **250g**. At the 50g base fruit value, the dried batch formula gives **400g**: 7.5 × 50 + 25. The added value is **150g for the whole batch**, before Artisan.

That example uses five matching normal-quality inputs. Five higher-quality berries give the same ordinary dried sale value but surrender a larger raw sale. If your main problem is several chests of berries, the next-morning batch can be useful even though a different machine may earn more per individual berry. Count both the backlog and your free machine slots.
