Use a Rain Totem for a job that needs tomorrow's rain, not because you want more Battery Packs. In 1.6, the old technique of chaining stormy days with a totem was fixed. Rain and lightning are different requirements.

## Check the date and the region first

A Rain Totem affects **the next day** in the region where you use it. Mainland and Ginger Island weather can differ. Using a totem on the mainland is not a way to prepare an island-only rainy-day activity.

It also cannot override a mainland festival or the first day of a season. The [Rain Totem reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Rain_Totem) lists the exceptions and the 1.6 storm change.

Before using one, answer three questions: where is the activity, is tomorrow an eligible date, and does rain actually satisfy the missing condition? A fish can still require a season, time window, or location that the totem does not change.

## The recipe is a real material commitment

Crafting unlocks at **Foraging level 9**, using **1 Hardwood, 1 Truffle Oil, and 5 Pine Tar**. A totem is consumed on use.

The Pine Tar supply comes from the appropriate [tapped tree](/guides/tapper-tree-products-guide/), while Truffle Oil has a different production chain. Owning the recipe does not mean that a totem is the cheapest way to solve every watering problem.

For example, two planned rainy days made with two totems require ten Pine Tar and two Truffle Oil, plus the hardwood. Decide whether both days are useful before consuming a reserve that another project needs.

## Fishing: prepare the full attempt

Check the target's season, water, time, and rain requirement. If weather is the only missing condition, a totem can make the next outing easier to schedule.

Pack the rod, compatible attachments, food, and free inventory space before the weather day arrives. Using materials to produce rain and then spending the morning shopping for tackle wastes part of the opportunity you created.

Our [bait and tackle guide](/guides/bait-and-tackle-guide/) helps match equipment to the catch problem. The [legendary fish guide](/guides/legendary-fish-guide/) also distinguishes weather eligibility from actually landing a difficult fish.

Do not count tomorrow's catch as guaranteed income. The totem changes the weather condition, not the result of every cast.

## Watering-can hand-ins still need a collection plan

Rain can cover a day when the can is at Clint's, provided the field is outdoors and affected by that weather. The [tool upgrade guide](/guides/tool-upgrades-guide/) shows the hand-in sequence.

Water today before leaving the can, confirm tomorrow's weather plan, and check that you can collect the tool the following day. Indoor crop watering and other unaffected chores need a separate arrangement.

Compare using a totem with simply waiting for a forecast rainy day. If there is no deadline, keeping the materials may be the easier choice. If a crop schedule is tight and the weather plan is valid, spending the totem can be reasonable.

## Batteries require lightning

A rainy day does not promise a storm. Do not multiply “one totem” by the number of Lightning Rods and budget that many batteries for the next morning.

Set up [rods](/guides/lightning-rods-battery-packs-guide/) before natural storms and count the packs actually produced. If a battery project is waiting, choose a different task rather than spending a chain of Rain Totems based on pre-1.6 advice.

This distinction is useful beyond the totem: a recipe might depend on weather, a region, or an event that looks similar without using the same underlying condition.

## Save an unnecessary use

Read tomorrow's forecast before consuming a totem when the forecast is available. If it already meets the goal, keep the item. Then check the calendar once more and place the remaining ingredients back in storage rather than carrying them through the outing.

For ordinary farm care, [sprinklers](/guides/sprinkler-guide/) can remove repeated watering work without consuming a weather item every time. The totem is most useful when the particular day matters.

## A three-day watering-can hand-in

| Day | Action | What covers outdoor watering |
|---|---|---|
| Today | Water first, use the totem if needed, hand the can to Clint | The can before hand-in |
| Tomorrow | Do another activity | Valid mainland rain |
| Following day | Collect the finished can, then water | The returned can |

Check shop access on the collection day before committing. A Greenhouse or indoor pots are not watered by mainland rain, so make another arrangement for those plants. A natural rainy forecast can support the same hand-in without spending a totem. This schedule solves one interrupted tool day, not every future watering chore.
