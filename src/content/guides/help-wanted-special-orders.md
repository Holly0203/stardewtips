Read the verbs before accepting a request. “Deliver this item,” “collect these materials,” and “ship this harvest” can use different counters. A chest full of old stock may help with delivery while doing nothing for a requirement to gather new items after acceptance.

## Two boards, different time limits

The Help Wanted board outside Pierre's offers short requests. Once accepted, a request normally allows **the current day and the next day**. Some story quests have no expiration, so check the actual Journal entry rather than treating every task as the same two-day job.

Town Special Orders become available through the board introduced from **Fall 2, Year 1**. New choices appear on Mondays. Their remaining time decreases from the posting period even if you accept later in the week.

These rules are explained in the [Quests reference](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Quests). Do not read a seven-day maximum as seven fresh days granted whenever you decide to click it.

## Test whether the request fits the next two days

Before accepting a short delivery, check three things: whether the item is available, whether you can reach the recipient, and whether the reward is worth the item and travel.

If the item is already stored and a normal errand passes the recipient, the request can be convenient. If it needs a particular rainy fish and no suitable window remains, accepting it does not create the weather or extend the timer.

Our [fishing guide](/guides/fishing-guide-beginners/) explains availability conditions. A request naming a fish is still subject to the source and catch requirements in your save.

## Delivery and gathering use different progress

An ordinary item delivery can use an existing item. A gathering request needs new collection after acceptance. Talking to the requestor and handing over an item are also different completion actions; follow the Journal's instruction for that type.

For a Special Order with both collection and deposit requirements, inspect both counters. Depositing an old reserve may satisfy one part while newly collecting the quantity is still necessary.

Do a small valid action and check the counter if the wording is unclear. That is less costly than gathering or donating an entire stack under the wrong interpretation.

## Use the displayed deadline in a realistic plan

Suppose an order needs forty new items and shows four days remaining. The rough target is ten per day. That arithmetic does not establish that your source can produce ten; compare it with the harvest or gathering rate you actually have.

If the current field will produce only once before the deadline, count that harvest rather than imagining several future plantings. If a recipient or deposit location has restricted access, allow time for the final step as well.

The [crop planning guides](/guides/summer-crop-guide/) and [mine trip guide](/guides/mining-guide-beginners/) can help estimate whether a requested activity fits. A prestigious reward is not a reason to ignore a schedule that cannot work.

## Keep request items out of ordinary processing

Make a small reserve once the job is accepted. A requested fish should not become smoked stock unless the order explicitly accepts that product. A crop needed in a particular quality should not disappear into a recipe.

Use the [storage guide](/guides/backpack-storage-guide/) to separate the reserve from current machine inputs. After completion or failure, return leftover materials to their normal categories so the quest chest does not keep collecting unrelated items.

If the job requires shipping, follow that instruction instead of selling to a counter for immediate money. The [shipping guide](/guides/shipping-bin-or-shop/) explains why ordinary sales and shipping credit are separate records.

## Declining a bad fit is reasonable

Short Help Wanted requests have no penalty simply for being accepted and not completed. That does not mean repeatedly buying expensive ingredients to rescue unsuitable tasks is necessary.

Take requests that add something useful to a day you wanted to play. If an order would require a week of an activity you dislike, let it wait for another appearance or choose a different project. The board can support the farm without setting the entire schedule.
