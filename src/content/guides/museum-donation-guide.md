Check an unfamiliar artifact or mineral’s description before selling it. If Gunther still needs it, put it with the next town-trip items. Keep duplicate resources separately so the donation pile stays small enough to check quickly.

A simple sorting system makes the collection easier to grow and leaves more attention for the rest of the farm.

> **At a glance**
>
> - Gunther accepts artifacts and minerals, with one donation per distinct item.
> - An undonated item's description identifies its Museum use.
> - Group likely donations for a town trip instead of making a trip for every find.
> - Duplicates may still have crafting or collection-project value.
> - Claim available rewards after donating; check seed seasons before planting them.

## Use the description before relying on memory

The [Museum reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Museum) explains the inventory description that indicates a possible donation. Read it when sorting a mining haul. This is more dependable than trying to remember whether a similar-looking stone is already on a display table.

Create a small “take to town” reserve. Move likely new donations there as you unpack, and carry them when the next trip is already useful. Once the reserve is familiar, checking it becomes part of preparing for town rather than a separate collection chore.

If the item is ambiguous, inspect it again before selling. A short pause in storage can prevent the much longer job of searching for a replacement.

## Keep duplicates separate from new finds

Only one copy of a particular artifact or mineral can be donated. After the first donation, decide what another copy is for. It might be a sale, a gift, a recipe ingredient, or a reserve for a project you can name.

“Already donated” therefore does not automatically mean “safe to discard.” Equally, an item does not need an unlimited stack merely because it was once useful. A small purposeful reserve avoids both extremes.

Our [backpack and storage guide](/guides/backpack-storage-guide/) explains how to separate current-trip items from longer-term supplies. The Museum reserve should reduce travel, not become a reason to carry the entire collection on every outing.

## Make a town loop around discoveries

A useful sequence is to process the geodes you budgeted for, inspect the results, then take eligible new items to Gunther. Leave room in the backpack for rewards before beginning the loop.

Do not spend all your money opening geodes simply because the Museum is nearby. Choose a processing budget first. Our [geode guide](/guides/geode-processing-guide/) explains why collecting a pile and paying to open it are separate resource decisions.

Check available hours and festival restrictions before starting the trip. If ordinary services are unavailable, keep the reserve safely stored and choose another task rather than standing in town with a bag full of items you cannot use.

## Track milestones without forcing the whole collection

The Museum contains **95 donatable items**. At **60 donations**, Gunther delivers the Rusty Key the next day; completing the collection rewards a Stardrop. The linked reference also lists item-specific and intermediate rewards.

Use milestones as direction, not a reason to abandon everything else. If your next several missing items come from activities you do not currently have access to, building the farm's capabilities may be a better use of the day than repeating the same unsuccessful search.

When you do claim a reward, inspect what it is and decide where it belongs. A seed packet is not necessarily ready to plant in the current season. Store it with the relevant seasonal plan instead of putting it immediately into the soil because it was free.

## Treat unusual first finds as a decision point

Some items have important uses beyond donation. When you find something rare for the first time, read its specific entry and your active goals before choosing its destination. Do not apply “always donate the first one” without considering what you are delaying.

 Reserve the extra attention for finds that are difficult to replace or clearly connected to another unlock.

If your larger priority is bundles, compare those needs in the [Community Center guide](/guides/community-center-guide/) as well. The Museum and Community Center are separate systems, and progress in one does not automatically satisfy the other.

## Arrange displays for readability

You can rearrange donated items through the Museum interface. Grouping minerals or artifacts in a way you understand can make visual checking easier, but decoration is optional. Do not let a perfect display plan stop you from donating a useful find.

If you are close to completion, use the collection reference to check specific missing items rather than relying only on empty-looking display spaces. Not every available space represents a missing donation.

For steady new discoveries, return to a manageable [mining routine](/guides/mining-guide-beginners/) and bring the results home safely. Collection progress tends to be easier to maintain when it fits around activities you already enjoy.

**References:** donation rules, description behavior, rearranging, and milestone totals checked against the [Museum Wiki entry](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Museum) on 24 October 2026. Trip planning assumes an unmodded 1.6 game.
