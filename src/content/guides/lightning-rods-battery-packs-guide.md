Build Lightning Rods before a storm, then collect the packs afterward. Rods do not have a sprinkler-style protection radius, so place them somewhere convenient on the farm. Several unused rods provide more capacity than one rod that has already caught a strike.

> **At a glance**
>
> - The recipe unlocks at **Foraging level 6**.
> - Each rod needs **1 Iron Bar, 1 Refined Quartz, and 5 Bat Wings**.
> - Rods intercept lightning on the farm, not within a placement-based radius.
> - A charged rod cannot absorb another strike that day.
> - Rain alone is not a promise of batteries; you need lightning.

## Prepare the three ingredients separately

Iron, quartz, and monster drops come from different parts of your early progression. Keep a small crafting reserve instead of selling everything from a mine trip. If you lack one ingredient, identify it before setting off; the [mining guide](/guides/mining-guide-beginners/) can help you make the trip purposeful.

For example, four rods require **4 Iron Bars, 4 Refined Quartz, and 20 Bat Wings** using the recipe above. That is a sample batch, not a recommended universal minimum. Your first affordable group is more useful than waiting for an enormous arrangement you cannot finish before the next storm.

Refined Quartz can also come from recycling Broken Glasses and Broken CDs. Our [recycling guide](/guides/recycling-machine-guide/) explains that route if your fishing chest has become a pile of overlooked materials.

## Understand what placement changes

According to the [Lightning Rod reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Lightning_Rod), a rod's location on the farm does not determine a local protection area. You can therefore choose an accessible location instead of surrounding every crop patch individually.

A good spot leaves walking space and makes charged rods easy to see. Avoid placing them where you will later need a gate, a building footprint, or a regular route. Grouping rods is useful for collection, even though the grouping itself does not improve interception.

Off-farm rods do not produce Battery Packs from lightning. If a decorative setup is outside the farm, do not count it when planning your battery supply.

## Why one rod is not enough for repeated strikes

Once struck, a rod processes its charge and cannot intercept additional strikes for the rest of that day. A storm can produce multiple strikes, so unused capacity matters. The proportion of already charged rods also affects interception chances.

That makes “I built one, so everything is safe” an unreliable assumption. Multiple rods improve available capacity, but no small fixed number is a guarantee against every storm. Avoid guides that promise a permanently safe farm from a precise arrangement without explaining those limits.

You do not have to stand beside the rods waiting for lightning. Continue your day's work; return to collect the resulting packs when ready. A rod that intercepted a strike produces its Battery Pack the following day.

## Keep the first batteries for named projects

Before selling your first pack, inspect the recipes and projects you expect to need. Set aside a reserve for something concrete, such as the next sprinkler upgrade or a later repair. A reserve with a purpose is easier to manage than keeping every item simply because it seems rare.

Use our [sprinkler guide](/guides/sprinkler-guide/) to decide whether higher-tier watering is actually your next constraint. If you cannot yet supply the other materials, storing batteries is still reasonable, but building more rods may not be the most urgent use of iron.

 Do not dismantle a working plan because you assume another batch will arrive tomorrow.

## A storm-day routine without waiting around

Before the storm season, finish the rods and leave them in place. During a storm, check ordinary farm tasks and use the time for your chosen activity. Afterward, collect completed packs and put them in the designated reserve.

If nothing appears, ask three questions: was there actual lightning, were the rods on the farm, and had they already been holding charges? This is more useful than repeatedly moving them closer to crops.

Storms do not occur during Winter on the main farm, so make a winter project list using the stock you actually have. If the supply is short, choose another task from our [Winter guide](/guides/winter-guide/) while working toward an alternative source or the next suitable season.

## Expand for capacity, not symmetry

Add rods when you have useful demand and can afford the inputs. An empty decorative row provides no extra value until it catches a strike, and a beautiful arrangement can still be awkward to collect.

Start with accessible equipment, observe the supply, and expand if batteries are repeatedly holding back your plans.
