Sell the wine that will not fit in your cellar unless you have a reason to reserve it. Aging is an extra step after processing, not a requirement before wine becomes saleable. A small cellar cannot age every bottle from a large keg room at the same rate the room produces it.

## Casks need the cellar

The final farmhouse upgrade provides a cellar and the Cask recipe. A cask uses **20 Wood and 1 Hardwood**. Although you can place one elsewhere, it only accepts goods for aging in the cellar.

Wine, Cheese, Goat Cheese, Beer, Pale Ale, and Mead are accepted products. Juice is not on that list. The [Cask reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Cask) documents compatibility and timing.

If you are still deciding whether to make wine at all, start with the [keg comparison](/guides/keg-vs-preserves-jar/). Buying the cellar does not create fruit or replace the machine needed to produce the initial bottle.

## Compare the value added by aging

Ordinary Wine takes fourteen days to reach silver, twenty-eight total to gold, and fifty-six total to iridium. The corresponding multipliers are **1.25, 1.5, and 2** of the ordinary wine price.

For a hypothetical bottle worth **1,000g before aging**:

| Collection point | Sale price | Added by aging |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ordinary | 1,000g | — |
| Silver, day 14 | 1,250g | 250g |
| Gold, day 28 | 1,500g | 500g |
| Iridium, day 56 | 2,000g | 1,000g |

The last row is an extra 1,000g, not 2,000g earned by the cask. The original bottle could have been sold immediately. At these assumptions, the added value averages about **17.9g per cask-day** over the full cycle.

For a different wine, substitute its ordinary sale value. Products with different aging schedules need their own calculation; do not apply the wine timetable to Cheese.

## Keep operating money outside the cellar

A fifty-six-day batch spans two seasons. Before filling every cask, keep enough income moving to pay for seeds, food, and any planned buildings. Aging potential cannot cover a purchase that is due before collection.

One workable policy is to reserve only the next cellar load and sell the rest of each keg harvest. That prevents a small set of casks from creating an ever-growing warehouse of bottles waiting their turn.

If you already have more waiting wine than the next cycle needs, ask why it is being kept. A future expansion is a reason only when you intend to build it and can explain the timing.

## Leave paths you can actually use

An accessible layout gives fewer casks than a room packed wall to wall, but reduces the work of loading and collecting. A packed layout requires moving equipment to reach the back. That trade is most noticeable when you interrupt aging or switch products frequently.

Try the supplied room before crafting a large additional batch. Walk to each cask, load it, and check whether your platform's interaction controls reach the corners you planned to use.

Our [storage guide](/guides/backpack-storage-guide/) can help prepare enough backpack space for a cellar collection. Arriving with a nearly full bag turns a quiet periodic job into several unnecessary trips.

## Can you take a product out early?

Yes. A tool can remove a product before maximum quality, as described in the reference. The star on the cask indicates its current stage. Check that stage before acting so a planned gold collection does not become an early normal-quality retrieval.

Early collection can be reasonable when cash is needed or you want the slot for something else. It also ends the current aging commitment, so compare the next use rather than repeatedly interrupting every batch out of impatience.

## When the cellar can wait

If seed money is tight or your [Greenhouse](/guides/greenhouse-guide/) and kegs are still being supplied, finish those working systems first. The cellar improves the value of a limited subset of finished goods; it is not the first step in creating a dependable crop-processing routine.

**Reference:** accepted products, cellar restriction, and aging stages checked against the linked Cask entry on 24 October 2026. The 1,000g bottle and cask-day calculation are examples without additional modifiers.
