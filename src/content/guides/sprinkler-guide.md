Hand-watering is the single biggest time sink in early Stardew Valley — every crop you plant steals minutes from your evening, and a missed day pushes your harvest one day closer to the season's end. Sprinklers remove that tax permanently. Here's how each type works, the levels that unlock them, and the exact grid spacing that waters every tile.

> **At a glance**
>
> - Sprinkler recipes unlock at **Farming level 2, 6, and 9** — growing crops, not mining, is what teaches them.
> - Sprinklers **never water the tile they sit on** — place them on paths, not in your crop rows.
> - Basic waters 4 tiles, Quality waters 8 (a full 3×3 ring), Iridium waters 24 (a full 5×5 ring).
> - Rainy days water crops anyway and **refill nothing** — sprinklers work identically in sun and storm.
> - A Quality Sprinkler every 3 tiles, or an Iridium every 5 tiles, waters 100% of your field with zero dry gaps.

None of them are sold in shops — this is a **crafter's game**. You mine the bars (our [mining guide for beginners](/guides/mining-guide-beginners/) covers smelting fast), and the Traveling Cart sometimes stocks ready-made Sprinklers and Quality Sprinklers if you want a head start on cash. Refined Quartz is just ![Quartz](/images/items/quartz.png) smelted in a furnace — the step most people forget is available on day one of mining.

## Which one to build, and when

**Basic Sprinkler (Lv 2):** your first craft is worth it the moment you have a 15+ tile field. It waters the four neighbors, so alternating sprinklers and crop rows cuts watering time dramatically even though the diagonal corners need hand-watering. Cheap copper + iron, immediate payback.

**Quality Sprinkler (Lv 6): the workhorse.** Eight tiles including diagonals — a perfect 3×3 block around each unit, so they tile the entire plane with no awkward gaps. This is the sprinkler most players should rush: a full early farm can run on six to eight of them.

**Iridium Sprinkler (Lv 9):** endgame automation. One unit plus its tile effectively services 25 squares, and iridium only appears deep in the Skull Cavern — the [Skull Cavern guide](/guides/skull-cavern-guide/) doubles as your battery-and-iridium roadmap.

## Grid layouts that water everything

- **Quality:** place one on every 3rd row and every 3rd column (spacing 3). Each 3×3 cell = 8 crops + 1 sprinkler. No dry tile exists.
- **Iridium:** spacing 5 — one sprinkler per 5×5 cell (24 crops). Fewest units, largest tilled area per walk.
- **Basic:** spacing 2 in a checkerboard if you're desperate early, but you're better off saving copper for Quality.
- **Put sprinklers on path tiles.** Since they never water themselves, a sprinkler standing on a walkway costs zero farmland — build rows of 8 crops between paths of sprinklers.

## Things sprinklers don't do

- **They don't stop crows** — scarecrows are a separate placement puzzle, covered in the [farm layout guide](/guides/farm-layout-tips/).
- **They don't work on untiled soil** — obviously — and they only water the standard tile layer; trees and forage are unaffected.
- **They don't upgrade by crafting in place** — swap the item yourself, or buy the Pressure Nozzle/Enricher upgrades from Qi later.
- The **Greenhouse** is the one place coverage is free forever: border sprinklers water the whole 120-tile plot. Layout in the [greenhouse guide](/guides/greenhouse-guide/).

## Tips to switch over smoothly

1. **Craft 4–8 sprinklers before expanding your field** — till first, place sprinklers, then plant around them.
2. **Keep one row hand-watered** as a test strip when changing layouts, so you can see the pattern at work.
3. **Upgrade your can anyway** — sprinklers cover crops, but mining days and sapling planting still want water on hand ([tool upgrades guide](/guides/tool-upgrades-guide/)).
4. **Winter craft time**: with no crops to water, the cold months are the ideal season to smelt bars and pre-build a whole year's sprinklers — see the [winter guide](/guides/winter-guide/).

## The bottom line

Sprinklers are the closest thing Stardew has to buying back real hours. Rush Farming levels by planting, smelt iron and gold early, and switch to Quality Sprinklers at level 6 — from that day your daily routine is: harvest, ship, go do something fun.
