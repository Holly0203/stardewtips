Save one promising item from several activities before Fall 16: a good crop, a fish, a mineral, an artisan product. A varied nine-item display is a better starting point than nine expensive items from the same category.

You do not need the theoretical maximum score to have a good Fair. A deliberate nine-item selection is a better first goal than trying to rebuild the entire farm around one event.

> **At a glance**
>
> - The Fair takes place on **Fall 16**.
> - Enter Pelican Town between **9am and 3pm**.
> - Bring **nine individual display items** and aim for varied categories.
> - First place requires **90 points**.
> - Retrieve your display after judging; items are not returned automatically.

## Start a reserve before the morning of the Fair

When you harvest or find something promising, put one suitable item aside. A small Fair reserve lets you compare options later instead of discovering on Fall 16 that every strong candidate was already sold or processed.

Keep the reserve distinct from items you must not lose for bundles or another project. Displayed items can be collected afterward, but packing and retrieving them still require attention.

Our [storage guide](/guides/backpack-storage-guide/) explains how to prepare a temporary event chest without disrupting ordinary resource storage. You do not need to carry the candidates during every outing before the event.

## Fill every slot before fine-tuning

The [Fair scoring reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Stardew_Valley_Fair) describes four components: a **14-point base**, an item-count contribution of **2 × items − 9**, category points, and each item's quality/value score.

For nine items, the count contribution is nine points. That is the reason to complete the display before obsessing over a marginal substitution. An empty slot affects the whole structure of the score.

Lay out the nine candidates at home first. Check that they are nine items you actually mean to display rather than assuming a stack or a valuable-looking collection automatically fills the table the way you expect.

## Variety can outweigh a near-duplicate

The game recognizes eight categories: Animal Products, Artisan Goods, Cooking, Fish, Foraging, Fruits, Minerals, and Vegetables. Each represented category adds five points, capped at thirty, so six categories maximize that component.

That encourages a display built from different farm activities. Two similar expensive vegetables may not improve the score as much as replacing one with a strong item from a category you have not represented yet.

You do not need to build an entire new production system for a single display slot. First look at what your farm already has: catches, forage, minerals, saved crops, and processed goods. Use the linked scoring table for exact item classifications, since game categories can differ from everyday intuition.

## Use a score budget instead of a promised winning list

With nine items and six categories, the base, item-count, and category components total **53 points**. Reaching ninety then needs **37 points from the individual items**.

As a hypothetical example, eight items worth four points each and one worth five contribute thirty-seven. This shows how the target is assembled; it does not claim that any arbitrary item with a particular sale price has those points.

Check each candidate's actual quality and the reference's price bands before counting it. If your save uses modified profit margins or different sale bonuses, do not assume an online example transfers unchanged.

## Improve the weakest decision first

Once the display is full and varied, compare your weakest item with alternatives already available. A better-quality fish, a useful mineral, or an artisan product may provide a straightforward improvement without a last-minute farm overhaul.

Our [fishing guide](/guides/fishing-guide-beginners/) and [keg and jar guide](/guides/keg-vs-preserves-jar/) explain those production activities outside the event. Use outputs you can realistically obtain rather than relying on a perfect rare find the night before.

Keep at least one fallback in the reserve until you pack. That makes it easier to replace a candidate that turns out to belong to a different category than expected.

## Finish the event in the right order

Check the display before asking Lewis to judge. Afterward, collect the result and decide how to use your Star Tokens at the event. Do not assume tokens saved at the end will carry into next year's Fair.

Before leaving, return to the display and retrieve every item. The Lost and Found provides recovery for forgotten items, but collecting them now is simpler than creating another errand.

If your display did not win, identify whether the shortfall came from empty slots, variety, or individual items. That gives next year's reserve a purpose instead of a vague instruction to bring “better stuff.”
