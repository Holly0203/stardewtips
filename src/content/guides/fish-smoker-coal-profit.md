Smoke the valuable catches first when coal is scarce. A Fish Smoker doubles a fish's sale value while preserving its quality, but each operation also consumes coal. The difference between a cheap catch and a valuable one is therefore important even though both occupy the same machine for the same time.

## What you need before the first batch

Willy sells the recipe for **10,000g**. Crafting a smoker requires **10 Hardwood and one each of Sea Jelly, River Jelly, and Cave Jelly**. Loading it uses **one fish and one Coal**, with a processing time of **50 in-game minutes**. A Riverland Farm starts with a smoker, so check your existing equipment before budgeting for the recipe.

Those requirements are listed in the [Fish Smoker reference](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Fish_Smoker). The recipe purchase unlocks crafting; it does not include the ingredients or provide a machine by itself.

If fishing is still difficult, buying the recipe is unlikely to be the next useful improvement. A smoker needs a regular supply of catches. Start with [bar control and rod choices](/guides/fishing-guide-beginners/), then look at processing when fish are coming home reliably.

## Compare the added value, not the final price

Let **F** be the raw fish's sale value at its actual quality, and **C** be the cost you assign to one Coal. Without profession bonuses, the simple added value is:

```
smoking gain = 2F − F − C = F − C
```

Here are hypothetical catches using an illustrative **150g coal cost**. This is a comparison assumption, not a claim about every coal source or year.

| Raw fish value | Smoked value | Added value after 150g coal |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 75g | 150g | −75g |
| 200g | 400g | 50g |
| 500g | 1,000g | 350g |

If you gathered the coal, you may choose a lower cash cost. It still has another use: smelting bars or crafting machines. That alternative is worth considering when your first tool upgrades are waiting for fuel.

Profession bonuses change the sale calculation, so use prices relevant to your own save rather than applying this no-profession table unchanged.

## Recovering the recipe purchase takes several sales

Suppose each smoked fish adds **250g after fuel** under your chosen assumptions. Dividing the 10,000g recipe price by 250 gives **40 operations** to cover the recipe payment alone. Construction materials, travel, and the money tied up before sale are outside that calculation.

The example is useful when deciding whether to buy now. If you only fish occasionally, those forty operations may take a while. If you already have a smoker or intend to craft several, the one-time recipe payment has a different role in the decision.

Do not count the full smoked sale as money earned by the machine. You already owned a fish that could have been sold raw. Only the extra value, after fuel, helps repay the new expense.

## Sort the fishing haul before loading

First reserve anything needed for a bundle, collection display, or other project. Keep the exact requested item intact rather than assuming a smoked version will substitute. Then compare the remaining catches by their actual raw sale value.

With a limited coal reserve, process the catches that add the most value under your assumptions. Sell low-value overflow directly if keeping it would tie up fuel needed elsewhere. The [shipping and shop guide](/guides/shipping-bin-or-shop/) explains the separate question of when you receive the money.

A small chest beside the smoker is enough for a chosen queue. Avoid moving every fish into it by default; the queue should reflect a decision, not merely become another place to postpone sorting.

## Why the smoker is sitting idle

Check that a suitable fish and coal are both available. If the inputs are ready but you keep forgetting collections, move the machine onto a route you use. A fifty-minute cycle does not require standing beside it, but it does require noticing when a batch finishes.

If fuel repeatedly runs out, compare the smoker with your [furnace budget](/guides/furnace-and-coal-planning/). Adding another smoker will not fix the same missing coal supply.

**Reference:** recipe, inputs, timing, and quality retention checked against the linked Wiki entry on 24 October 2026. All break-even figures above are worked examples without professions; substitute your own fuel and sale values.
