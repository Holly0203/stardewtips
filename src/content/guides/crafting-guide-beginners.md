An early chest is useful as soon as the backpack starts filling. A furnace becomes useful when ore comes home from the mines. Other recipes can wait until you have something for the machine to do. Start with the next job on the farm rather than trying to build every unlocked recipe.

> **At a glance**
>
> - Open the menu with the **hammer icon** (or the `B` key) — recipes appear there as you unlock them.
> - There is no separate Crafting skill. Recipes come from skill levels, shops, friendship events, and other rewards.
> - Free from day one: ![Chest](/images/items/chest.png) Chest, ![Gate](/images/items/gate.png) Gate, ![Torch](/images/items/torch.png) Torch, ![Campfire](/images/items/campfire.png) Campfire.
> - Bring 1 ![Copper Ore](/images/items/copper-ore.png) copper ore up from the mine and Clint hands you the ![Furnace](/images/items/furnace.png) Furnace blueprint the next morning.
> - In 1.6, Charcoal Kiln unlocks at Foraging 2 and Tapper at Foraging 4. Check the current recipe rather than relying on an older unlock list.

## How recipes unlock

Three sources cover almost everything in year one:

- **Skill levels.** Harvest crops to raise Farming, swing the axe to push Foraging, mine to push Mining. Each level-up can unlock recipes tied to that skill — so your first Scarecrow comes with Farming level 1 and your first Sprinkler with Farming level 2.
- **Story triggers.** The Furnace is the big one: mine a single copper ore and Clint delivers the blueprint the next morning. Others unlock from friendship levels, shops, or Community Center rewards (a Bee House is the Fall Crops Bundle reward — see the [Community Center guide](/guides/community-center-guide/)).
- **Purchases.** Vendors also sell recipes. Receiving a finished machine as a bundle reward does not teach its crafting recipe.

Locked recipes stay hidden, so don't panic when the menu looks empty on day one — it fills up as you do.

## Four materials to keep in storage

| Material | Where it comes from | Best early use |
|----------|--------------------|----------------|
| ![Wood](/images/items/wood.png) Wood | Chopping trees and stumps | Chests, fences, almost every machine |
| ![Stone](/images/items/stone.png) Stone | Breaking rocks on the farm and in the mines | Furnace, campfires, preserves jars |
| ![Fiber](/images/items/fiber.png) Fiber | Cutting weeds (free with a scythe) | Scarecrows and campfires |
| ![Coal](/images/items/coal.png) Coal | Rock nodes in the mine, Charcoal Kiln | Smelting, Bee Houses, Preserves Jars |

A first Chest uses 50 Wood. Stop clearing once the next useful craft is funded; chopping the entire farm can use the energy you wanted for fishing or mining.

These are the recipes worth hitting first, with the required materials:

| Scarecrow | 50 Wood + 20 Fiber + 1 Coal | Farming 1 | Stops crows from eating your crops |
| Furnace | 20 Copper Ore + 25 Stone | Clint cutscene | Smelts 5 ore + 1 coal into a metal bar |
| Sprinkler | 1 Copper Bar + 1 Iron Bar | Farming 2 | Waters the 4 tiles around it daily |

| Charcoal Kiln | 20 Wood + 2 Copper Bar | Foraging 2 | Wood becomes coal — moved down from level 4 in 1.6 |
| Mayonnaise Machine | 15 Wood + 15 Stone + 1 Earth Crystal + 1 Copper Bar | Farming 2 | Eggs into mayo once you have chickens |
| Bee House | 40 Wood + 8 Coal + 1 Iron Bar + 1 Maple Syrup | Farming 3 | Passive honey income in every season but winter |
| Tapper | 40 Wood + 2 Copper Bar | Foraging 4 | Sap, syrup, and resin off your trees — level changed from 3 in 1.6 |
| Preserves Jar | 50 Wood + 40 Stone + 8 Coal | Farming 4 | Jelly and pickles; a big jump in crop value |

Two notes for the planning crowd: artisan machines (keg, preserves jar, cheese press) multiply the value of what you already grow, which is the core of the [year-one money guide](/guides/make-money-year-one/). Input quality does not automatically carry into the finished product. Save normal-quality crops for jars and kegs when higher-quality copies would earn more if sold directly.

## Smelt for the next batch of recipes

Every mid-tier recipe eventually asks for **bars**, so the pipeline matters: mine ore (common copper floors 2–39, iron 41–79, gold 81–119), smelt 5 ore + 1 coal in the Furnace, and the bars unlock Sprinklers, Quality Sprinklers, and eventually the [tool upgrades](/guides/tool-upgrades-guide/) at Clint's. Refined Quartz — needed for Quality Sprinklers and lightning rods — is just regular Quartz smelted with a coal. Full mine details are in the [mining guide](/guides/mining-guide-beginners/).

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Never crafting chests.** Your shipping bin and one cluttered box are not storage. Chests are the cheapest quality-of-life in the game.
- **Building fences you'll replace.** Wood fences decay; that's part of the design. Skip stone and iron fences until animals actually need them.
- **Ignoring the Charcoal Kiln.** In 1.6 it unlocks at Foraging 2 — one kiln turns your wood surplus into the coal that every machine wants.
- **Crafting without checking coverage.** More sprinklers only help if they water planted tiles; more scarecrows only help if some crops are outside the existing protected area.

## What to build after the basics

Once storage and smelting are covered, choose the machine for the job you already have: [sprinklers](/guides/sprinkler-guide/) for a field that takes too long to water, a [tapper](/guides/tapper-tree-products-guide/) for planned keg materials, or a [jar](/guides/keg-vs-preserves-jar/) for crops waiting in storage.
