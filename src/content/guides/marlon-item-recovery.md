After losing items to a health collapse, check Marlon's Item Recovery Service before starting another dangerous outing. You can recover **one listed item or stack**, not the entire lost inventory. A later health collapse replaces the old loss list, so another attempt can remove the choice you were still considering.

## Identify what actually happened

A health collapse and reaching 2am are different events. Losing health in a dangerous area can remove inventory items. The ordinary 2am penalty does not use the same item-loss rule.

The [Marlon reference](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Marlon) describes recovery for losses in the Mines, Quarry Mine, Skull Cavern, and Volcano Dungeon. If the day ended without a lost-item list, do not assume every missing-looking item must be waiting in his menu.

Check your own storage and current equipment before buying a replacement. A tool left at home, an item placed in a chest, and an item lost in combat are different problems.

## Read the full recovery list before selecting

The service normally charges according to the lost item's sale value, and the selected recovery is delivered by mail. The [Guild's service description](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Monster_Eradication_Goals) explains that selecting one entry means the other listed losses are not all coming back in later separate orders.

Do not click the cheapest row simply to see how the service works. First identify what each entry represents and whether it is one object or an entire stack.

Leave time to receive the item before planning a trip that depends on it. Paying the recovery fee is not the same as already having the equipment back on the hotbar.

## Replacement difficulty matters more than sale price alone

Consider a hypothetical list containing a common resource stack, a weapon you rely on, and an uncommon ingredient reserved for a project. The most expensive listed row may not be the hardest to replace.

Ask what each loss prevents. A resource stack might be gathered during ordinary work. A needed weapon could block safe progress. A seasonal item might delay a goal until its source is available again.

Choose from those consequences, then compare the fee with buying or gathering a replacement. This is a case where a very low shipping price can conceal substantial practical value.

Our [storage guide](/guides/backpack-storage-guide/) helps avoid carrying a whole long-term reserve into the next trip. If the item had no role in the outing, leaving it at home would have removed that particular risk.

## Do not overwrite an unresolved choice

You can leave the selection undecided until another qualifying loss replaces it. That is useful if you need time to find the recovery money, but it is not a reason to immediately repeat the same difficult encounter.

While arranging funds, choose safer farm work or an outing you can handle with the remaining equipment. The [Year 1 spending guide](/guides/make-money-year-one/) can help separate the recovery payment from seeds and food already needed.

Before returning to combat, settle the recovery choice or consciously accept that another health collapse could change the available list.

## Fix the part of the trip that failed

If healing food was buried in a lower inventory row, move it beside the weapon. If the last fights were taking too much health, return to a comfortable area or improve the loadout before going back. If the trip ran long, set a return point you can recognize.

Our [combat guide](/guides/combat-survival-guide/) covers those practical changes. Recovering the lost weapon does not make an unchanged unsafe routine safer by itself.

For a mine outing, a small [checkpoint goal](/guides/mining-guide-beginners/) can be enough. For Skull Cavern, remember that the lack of an elevator creates a different trip structure; use its [preparation guide](/guides/skull-cavern-guide/) rather than copying an ordinary mine schedule.

## Empty the next backpack deliberately

Take the equipment and recovery stack needed for that destination, leaving gifts, bundle reserves, building materials, and unrelated purchases in storage. Do not make yourself choose between valuable long-term items again simply because they never got unpacked.

A recoverable loss can still be frustrating. The useful next step is a specific change to food access, equipment, or the trip goal, not a promise to play perfectly from now on.

**References:** eligibility, one-entry limit, list replacement, and fee basis checked against Marlon and the linked Guild service entry on 24 October 2026. Individual book discounts or special item-return rules should be checked separately.
