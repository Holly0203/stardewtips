Check the poster in the Adventurer's Guild before repeating mine floors for a reward. It records categories and counts, while Gil handles the rewards. Killing whatever happens to appear can advance the list, but it is not the same as targeting the equipment you actually want.

## The poster is a different system from the Journal

Guild access follows the Initiation quest. Once inside, inspect the Monster Eradication Goals list near the counter and compare it with your progress. These are continuing targets, not the same as a short Help Wanted slaying request.

The [Guild reference](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Monster_Eradication_Goals) lists eligible monsters and rewards. A creature that looks similar may belong to a different category, so check the definition when a count does not move as expected.

You can work toward a Guild goal during other trips. There is no need to abandon ore gathering solely because you want the next ring eventually.

## Choose the reward for a problem you have

A few examples show how different the objectives are:

| Goal | Required count | Reward |
|---|---:|---|
| Dust Sprites | 500 | Burglar's Ring |
| Rock Crabs | 60 | Crabshell Ring |
| Bats | 200 | Vampire Ring |
| Slimes in the qualifying category | 1,000 | Slime Charmer Ring |

The names and counts come from the linked checklist. Read the reward description before choosing the longest grind simply because its target number is large.

For a player frequently short of monster materials, a loot-oriented reward may be interesting. For someone learning dangerous encounters, survivability may be more relevant. Neither decision removes the need for food, weapon control, and a safe return route.

## Combine the count with a resource trip

Choose a floor band where both the target category and something else you need can appear. Then the outing can still contribute ore or materials even if the monster count is modest.

Write a practical stop condition: perhaps the next checkpoint, a food reserve threshold, or the material batch already planned. Do not assume a goal requiring hundreds of enemies should be finished in one day.

Our [mine checkpoint guide](/guides/mining-guide-beginners/) and [combat preparation guide](/guides/combat-survival-guide/) help keep the repeat visits manageable. A long-term equipment goal is easier to complete when individual trips remain recoverable.

## Count the remaining work, not the whole target again

If the poster shows 430 toward a target of 500, the remaining count is seventy. Track that difference when deciding whether a few targeted sessions are worthwhile.

For a planning example, seventy remaining enemies over five visits would require an average of fourteen per visit. That is a division exercise, not a promised spawn rate. If recent trips fall well short, adjust the timetable rather than treating every outing as a failure.

Check the poster occasionally instead of trying to maintain a perfect mental total while fighting. The game counter is more useful than an estimate based on how busy a floor looked.

## Make room before claiming the reward

When a goal is complete, talk to Gil with free inventory space. Do not arrive carrying every mining result and expect a reward exchange to solve the slot problem for you.

Inspect the new item's effect before replacing familiar equipment. A reward can support a different activity from the trip that earned it. Try the change on a comfortable outing before taking an unfamiliar loadout somewhere demanding.

## Keep collection progress from becoming a chore list

If the next reward does not solve a current problem, leave the goal running in the background. You can return when another recipe or exploration goal takes you through the appropriate area.

For harder outings, the [Skull Cavern guide](/guides/skull-cavern-guide/) covers separate preparation. A newly earned ring is one part of the equipment decision, not permission to ignore the differences between locations.

## Match the reward to an ordinary floor band

| Target | Useful ordinary source | Also gather |
|---|---|---|
| Dust Sprites | Floors 41–79 | Coal and Iron Ore |
| Rock Crabs | Early floors, commonly 1–29 | Copper and basic geodes |
| Skeletons | Floors 71–79 | Frozen-area materials |

Use the [monster list](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Monsters) to distinguish the ordinary and dangerous versions when planning a route. For a nearly finished target, count the remaining enemies from the poster rather than restarting the whole published goal in your head. An eligible source is an opportunity, not a guaranteed number of spawns per visit.

**Reference:** access, goal categories, counts, and reward names checked against the linked Guild page on 24 October 2026. The five-visit example is planning arithmetic; enemy appearances and results vary.
