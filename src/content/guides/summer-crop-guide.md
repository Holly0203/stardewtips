Blueberries keep producing after the first harvest; Melons need replanting; Starfruit needs a larger seed budget and access to the Oasis. Choose a mix you can water and harvest, then decide how much of it your machines can realistically process.

## Three common choices

| Crop | Seed cost | Base sale value | Initial growth | Later harvests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Blueberry | 80g | 50g per berry; at least 3 per pick | 13 days | Every 4 days |
| Melon | 80g | 250g | 12 days | Replant |
| Starfruit | 400g at Oasis | 750g | 13 days | Replant |

These are base values without quality or profession bonuses. The [Crops reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Crops) provides the full entries. A price comparison alone does not account for replanting money, machine capacity, or your daily watering time.

## Blueberries for repeated raw harvests

A daily-watered Blueberry planted on Summer 1 is ready on Summer 14, then produces on days 18, 22, and 26 without speed bonuses. Four picks of three normal-quality berries make a simple baseline of 600g gross from an 80g seed. That calculation excludes extra berries, quality, and modifiers.

This suits a field you intend to leave planted for the season. You still need to water and collect it. “Regrowing” saves the repeated seed purchase, not the morning chore.

Reserve any bundle crop before shipping the harvest. Do not assume the whole berry crop needs processing; a small set of machines may not have time to clear it all.

## Melons for a larger individual harvest

Melons pay in one batch and require new seeds after collection. That makes it easier to change the field's use after a harvest, but you need money available to replant if that is the plan.

Plant a block if you want to try for a Giant Melon, leaving the relevant mature crops in place. Giant crops are a chance-based extra, so do not build the necessary seed budget around a guaranteed giant result.

A Melon can become Wine in a Keg. The ordinary wine value is based on the fruit's base price; aging is a separate later process. The [keg and jar comparison](/guides/keg-vs-preserves-jar/) explains why the finished price is only part of that decision.

## Starfruit when the seed budget is ready

The Oasis sells Starfruit Seeds, which makes bus access part of the plan. At 400g each, even a modest field ties up more gold than Blueberries or Melons. Keep the replanting bill in mind before spending the first harvest on a building.

Wine processing adds another wait and another equipment requirement. If you need cash soon, selling some fruit directly can be reasonable even when a bottle would eventually sell for more.

Use the [Year 1 income guide](/guides/make-money-year-one/) to plan the interval between paying for seeds and receiving money back.

## Add variety for a reason

A few Tomatoes and Hot Peppers can support bundles or cooking; Corn continues into Fall; Hops can supply a Pale Ale project. Trellises need a layout that leaves the plants reachable. Our [field layout guide](/guides/farm-layout-tips/) covers that access problem.

Choose those additions according to an actual need instead of assuming every crop belongs in the first Summer field. A small varied plot can serve collection goals while the rest supplies straightforward harvests.

For flowers beside Bee Houses, keep the bloom standing and collect honey first. The [honey guide](/guides/bee-house-flower-honey-guide/) explains that separate use.

## Count backward from Summer 28

Without bonuses, thirteen-day crops need planting by Summer 15 for a final Summer 28 harvest. A twelve-day Melon can finish from a Summer 16 planting. Those dates assume no missed water and leave no safety margin.

Before planting another batch near the deadline, compare it with keeping money for Fall. A crop that cannot finish is a lost seed purchase. Corn and other explicitly multi-season crops need their own calendar rather than the blanket single-season rule.
