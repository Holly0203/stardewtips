Choose Fisher if you regularly sell fish caught with a rod. Consider Trapper if you are building a substantial Crab Pot route. At level 10, the choice becomes more specific: sale value or treasure on one branch, cleaner pot catches or no baiting on the other.

Instead of declaring one branch correct for every save, this guide maps the options to common goals and shows how to make a choice with your current farm in mind.

> **At a glance**
>
> - At level 5, **Fisher** improves fish sale value; **Trapper** reduces pot crafting costs.
> - Fisher leads to **Angler** or **Pirate** at level 10.
> - Trapper leads to **Mariner** or **Luremaster**.
> - Choose for the activities you perform regularly, not the largest number on the menu.

## Read the branch as two decisions

At level 5, Fisher makes fish worth **25% more**. Trapper changes the resources needed to craft Crab Pots. At level 10, the Fisher path offers Angler's **50% fish-value bonus** or Pirate's increased treasure chance. The Trapper path offers Mariner, which removes junk catches, or Luremaster, which removes the bait requirement.

Those descriptions come from the [Fishing skill reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Fishing). Angler's 50% is the resulting fish-value bonus, not an additional 50% to multiply on top of Fisher's 25%.

The first branch matters because it determines the later pair. Do not evaluate a level-5 convenience without looking at the level-10 options it makes available.

## Choose Fisher when rod fishing already pays your bills

If you take regular fishing trips and sell most catches, a sale bonus fits an activity you are already doing. You do not have to reorganize the farm to use it.

For a simplified example, fish worth a total of **1,000g before profession bonuses** become **1,250g with Fisher** or **1,500g with Angler**, ignoring item-by-item rounding and other modifiers. This is an arithmetic illustration, not an income forecast for a fishing day.

The size of the benefit still depends on how much you catch and sell. If you spend the following month focused entirely on crops, a theoretical strong bonus is not producing the same practical return as it did during your first Spring.

Our [Year 1 income guide](/guides/make-money-year-one/) can help place fishing in the larger farm budget rather than treating it as the only source of gold.

## Compare Angler with Pirate by the reward you want

Angler is straightforward when the goal is selling catches. Pirate shifts the attraction toward fishing treasure. More treasure opportunities are not a promise of the exact item you are missing.

Choose based on whether you enjoy collecting and opening treasure during the fishing you already do. If you mainly want reliable sale income, do not justify a treasure choice using the most exciting possible rare result alone.

Conversely, if ordinary income is already comfortable, collecting unusual finds can be a legitimate reason to prefer a different reward. The “best” choice changes when your goal changes from paying for seeds to enjoying the collection process.

## Trapper makes sense when the pot route is a real habit

Before selecting a pot-focused branch, run a small [Crab Pot route](/guides/crab-pot-route-guide/) for a while. Notice what takes time: the journey, inventory sorting, baiting, or unwanted output.

Reduced crafting inputs help when you are building pots, but that is not the same as a recurring payout on every day you own them. If most of your pots were already acquired another way and you do not plan to add more, consider how much of the crafting benefit remains relevant.

The branch is easier to evaluate when you can name the routine it supports. “I want a daily waterfront collection” is more useful than “I might eventually place pots everywhere.”

## Mariner and Luremaster solve different annoyances

Mariner addresses what you catch; Luremaster addresses how you prepare the next catch. Neither automatically removes the journey to collect the pots.

If you frequently leave harvested pots unbaited, removing junk does not fix that forgotten step. If you happily bait but dislike sorting recyclables, no-bait convenience may not target your main frustration.

Remember that unwanted output can also have a use. Our [recycling guide](/guides/recycling-machine-guide/) explains the resource route, which may affect how strongly you value removing junk.

## Make the choice from a recent week, not a distant plan

Look at your last several in-game days. How often did you fish with a rod? How many pots did you maintain? Did you sell fish, keep them for cooking, or spend the sessions pursuing collections?

Choose the benefit that aligns with those answers, or with a specific change you are ready to make now. Avoid reorganizing the entire farm solely to justify a choice made for an imaginary future routine.

If rod fishing is frustrating, a profession bonus is not the first control fix. Use our [bait and tackle guide](/guides/bait-and-tackle-guide/) to address the actual catch problem before judging the activity's rewards.
