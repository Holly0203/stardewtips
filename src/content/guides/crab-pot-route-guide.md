Put your first Crab Pots somewhere you already visit. Collecting and rebaiting a short row is easy to fit into the day; crossing the map for a few catches is a different commitment. Choose freshwater or ocean water according to the catches you need.

> **At a glance**
>
> - Crafting unlocks at **Fishing level 3**.
> - The standard recipe is **40 Wood and 3 Iron Bars** per pot.
> - Willy sells pots for **1,500g** after the level requirement is met.
> - Load bait, then check the following morning.
> - Freshwater and ocean locations have different catch pools.

## Choose a reason to collect

Start with the goal: bundle items, cooking ingredients, Fishing experience, a recycling supply, or a small stream of saleable catches. These goals can justify different locations and different route lengths.

If you need a particular catch, look at the freshwater and ocean lists in the [Crab Pot reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Crab_Pot) before placing equipment. “Near water” is not specific enough to determine every possible result.

For the first few pots, prefer a location you already pass. The marginal travel cost is what matters: a pot beside an existing stop is less work than one that requires its own round trip. If the catch you need requires a less convenient location, make that a deliberate temporary project.

## Compare building and buying

Three standard crafted pots need **120 Wood and 9 Iron Bars**. Buying three costs **4,500g** at the listed price. These totals show the resource trade, not which option is always cheaper: the value of iron depends on what you would otherwise build with it.

If those bars are reserved for essential machines or upgrades, buying a small number later may fit better. If money is tight but materials are already available, crafting may be reasonable. Do not count raw materials as free just because they came from the mines.

Our [tool upgrade guide](/guides/tool-upgrades-guide/) can help identify competing bar demands. The first route should fit around your farm's main project rather than consume the whole budget by accident.

## Separate collecting from refilling

After removing the catch, make sure the pot is prepared for another night. Without the Luremaster profession, that normally means adding bait again. A harvested but unbaited pot is simply equipment waiting for attention.

A reliable interaction order is to collect from one small group, refill it, then move to the next. This makes it easier to notice an empty pot than collecting an entire shoreline first and trying to remember which ones were finished.

Carry enough bait for the route, plus a little margin. For a hypothetical twelve-pot route checked once a day, plan twelve bait uses per complete refill. That is a workload estimate for your own route, not a guarantee of twelve valuable catches.

## Plan for the unwanted output too

Trash is a normal possible result, not evidence that the pot is broken. Keep a stack for processing instead of repeatedly deciding whether to throw each piece away. The [recycling guide](/guides/recycling-machine-guide/) explains what can become useful material.

A useful small arrangement is a catch chest near the route's end and recycling at a place you regularly visit. You do not need to carry the entire week's output on every trip. Sort new catches when you return, keeping bundle or recipe reserves separate from the shipping pile.

Our [backpack and storage guide](/guides/backpack-storage-guide/) covers the slot problem that often appears before the pot route itself becomes expensive.

## Professions change the chore you are accepting

The Trapper branch reduces crafting inputs and leads to two different conveniences: Mariner removes junk from pot catches, while Luremaster removes the bait requirement. Those solve different frustrations.

Ask which part of the routine you dislike. If refilling is the part you forget, removing junk will not fix that omission. If you want cleaner output, saving on bait does not make every result a fish. The [Fishing professions guide](/guides/fishing-professions-guide/) compares these choices with the rod-fishing branch.

Do not choose a whole profession path based on a route you have not yet tried. Run a few pots first and decide whether the activity deserves that much of your fishing strategy.

## Expand only after a complete week of use

Track whether you actually collected and refilled the initial group. If you skipped it repeatedly, adding more equipment will usually enlarge the same maintenance problem. Move the route, shorten it, or assign it a specific collection goal instead.

If the route is easy and the output has a purpose, add a modest batch. Check how the extra catches affect inventory space and recycling capacity. The best number is the one that still fits into your day without crowding out activities you prefer.
