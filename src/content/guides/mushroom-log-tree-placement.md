Put a Mushroom Log near wild trees, not in a fruit orchard. The surrounding tree count affects how many mushrooms it can produce, while the tree species influences the possible mushroom mix. More trees can improve the setup without making one particular mushroom guaranteed.

## A first log does not need a new forest

The recipe unlocks at **Foraging level 4** and requires **10 Hardwood and 10 Moss**. Before clearing an area or planting a large grid, look for a grove already on the farm with a convenient edge you can reach.

The [Mushroom Log reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Mushroom_Log) describes a **7 × 7 square** centered on the log. Wild trees in that square contribute to quantity, including immature ones. Fruit trees do not serve that role.

For the first test, count the nearby trunks and leave a direct approach to the log. A dense-looking forest is not useful if most of its trees are outside the square you are actually measuring.

## Quantity and species are separate questions

Outputs range from one to five mushrooms. The documented quantity rule needs **ten nearby trees to guarantee the five-item maximum**, while mushroom species is still selected randomly.

Do not interpret “five mushrooms” as “five Purple Mushrooms.” Mature oaks influence the pool toward Morels, pines toward Chanterelles, and other tree types have their own contributions. The normal distribution remains part of the selection.

That distinction matters when planning a recipe. A larger harvest provides more items to sort, but does not make a specific missing ingredient arrive on demand. Keep any needed example once it appears instead of assuming the next collection will replace it.

## Make the collection tile accessible

Start with one log, stand beside it, and check whether you can leave in the direction of your usual route. Then imagine neighboring trees fully grown. An arrangement that works while everything is a seedling can become awkward later.

You can combine the area with a [tapper grove](/guides/tapper-tree-products-guide/) if you leave room to reach both kinds of equipment. Do not copy a dense online layout before checking its interaction points on your platform.

For a small farm, accessible logs beside an existing grove are a sensible start. Planting a dedicated block becomes easier to justify after you know the mushrooms have a regular use.

## Allow for the collection cycle

The normal cycle is four days, with rainy days shortening it. Logs produce through all seasons. Check them when your route passes rather than applying an exact all-weather collection day to the whole season.

If several logs have different histories, they may not finish together. You can collect each when ready or deliberately align your own visiting routine; there is no need to wait beside the equipment for a shared batch.

Winter production is useful if it fits your [season plan](/guides/winter-guide/), but remember that the nearby grove has its own growth conditions. Planning around trees that are already established is different from expecting a newly planted Winter grove to be ready immediately.

## Sort edible output before processing

Read the item description before using a mushroom as recovery food. Not every mushroom is edible, and processors also have accepted-input rules. A red cap's visual appeal does not make it suitable for the same use as a Common Mushroom.

A [Dehydrator](/guides/dehydrator-five-item-batches/) can clear matching edible mushroom stacks, but needs five of the same type and quality. Smaller stacks can remain as ingredients or be sold if you have no current reason to keep them.

Avoid designing the whole grove around a hypothetical output price. The money you receive depends on the actual mix, the use you choose, and how consistently the setup is collected.

## Count trunks inside the actual seven-by-seven area

Use the log as the center and count three tiles out in each direction. A tree trunk just outside that square contributes nothing even when its canopy makes the grove look dense. Immature wild trees count toward quantity; fruit trees do not.

Ten contributing trees guarantee the documented five-mushroom quantity, but the species is still selected from the relevant pool. Plan a path to the log before planting the final trunks. If moving logs closer together, recheck each log's own square instead of assuming one nearby forest supplies every machine equally.
