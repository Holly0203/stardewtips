Count the seeds you need before loading the Seed Maker. Output varies, so process a small batch, check the packets, and continue until the planting is covered. For shop-bought seeds, compare the price with what the sacrificed crop could have earned.

> **At a glance**
>
> - Crafting unlocks at **Farming level 9**.
> - The recipe uses **25 Wood, 1 Gold Bar, and 10 Coal**.
> - A normal result is **one to three matching seeds**, but alternate results are possible.
> - Fruit-tree fruit does not turn into saplings in this machine.
> - Reserve the seeds needed for planting before processing more crops for sale.

## Begin with a seed problem, not a machine count

Ask what you need the Seed Maker to do. Are you expanding a rare crop, preparing a planting outside a shop's usual stock, or merely trying to save a little gold on ordinary seeds?

A rare seed supply can make conversion useful even when the sacrificed crop is valuable. With readily available shop seeds, the comparison is more direct: what could the crop earn elsewhere, and what would replacing those seeds cost?

The [Seed Maker reference](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Seed_Maker) documents the recipe and variable outputs. Start with one machine and a small reserve pile so you can inspect the result before committing the rest of a harvest.

## The input has an opportunity cost

Consider a hypothetical crop you could sell for **120g**. If a run gives two useful seeds, you have effectively exchanged that 120g for them: **60g per seed** before accounting for the machine. If the same seeds cost 40g each in a shop, that particular conversion did not save money.

If the run produces only one useful seed, the cost per seed is higher. If the crop could have generated a valuable processed product, its alternative use may be worth more than its raw shipping price. These examples illustrate a comparison; they are not a guaranteed output forecast.

Our [keg and jar guide](/guides/keg-vs-preserves-jar/) can help estimate that competing use. Compare realistic options you can access now, rather than assuming an enormous future processing room will handle every crop.

## Do not plan a precise field from a guaranteed average

Matching-seed output is variable, and a run can instead produce Mixed Seeds or an Ancient Seeds packet. That means a planning average is not a promise that the next crop will give exactly what your final empty tile needs.

For a time-sensitive planting, process your reserve early enough to count the actual seeds. Stop when the field's requirement is met, then decide what to do with the remaining produce. Leave a small margin if missing seeds would force an inconvenient shop trip.

For instance, a ten-tile expansion requires ten usable seeds, not “about five crops” on paper. After each small batch, count the packets you actually have.

## Regrowing crops change the replacement question

A crop that remains planted after harvest does not need a new seed for each pick. Before setting aside produce for seed production, work out whether you are replacing plants, expanding the field, or preparing for another season.

If the existing plants will keep producing and the field is already large enough, converting every harvest can leave you with packets you have no immediate use for. A defined expansion goal prevents the seed chest from becoming another unplanned stockpile.

Use the [Summer crop guide](/guides/summer-crop-guide/) to compare ongoing harvests with the space you want to keep planted. On the last days of a season, think about the next viable planting window before assuming every packet has immediate value.

## Some harvests are not suitable inputs

Fruit-tree produce is not accepted, so an orchard needs saplings rather than a Seed Maker loop. See our [fruit tree guide](/guides/fruit-tree-planting-guide/) for that separate system.

Other input exceptions also exist. When an item will not load, check the source's accepted-input list rather than crafting another machine. Similar-looking items are not necessarily part of the same processing rule.

Keep this distinction visible in storage: one stack for produce you intentionally plan to convert, another for ordinary selling and processing. Accidentally feeding a valuable reserved item into a machine is easier when every harvest shares one unlabeled pile.

## Compare a purchased crop with a rare expansion

For ordinary Parsnips, a 35g normal crop exchanged for one matching seed replaces a packet costing 20g; that particular result loses 15g of sale opportunity before machine cost. Two matching packets replace 40g of purchases. Output varies, so neither outcome is a promise for the next run.

For Ancient Fruit expansion, an easily purchased matching packet may not be available. Converting a small reserve can therefore serve access rather than shop savings. Keep enough fruit for the chosen expansion, process early, then count actual usable packets. Once the planted field is large enough, regrowth does not require another packet for every harvest.
