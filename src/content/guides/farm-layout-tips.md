A well-planned farm saves you time every single day — less walking, less watering, less mess. You don't need a perfect min-maxed layout; you just need a few smart habits.

## Start with zones

Think of your farm as a few dedicated areas:

- **Crop fields** — near your house and a water source.
- **Animal area** — for the Coop and Barn, with grass for grazing.
- **Buildings** — Silos, Sheds, and kegs grouped together.
- **Orchard** — fruit trees planted with space to grow.

Keeping these separate makes your daily routine a straight line instead of a maze.

## Crop fields

- **Leave room for sprinklers.** Plan your fields around sprinkler coverage so you don't have to redo them later.
- **Group crops by type** — it's easier to harvest and replant.
- **Put fields close to water** — fewer steps to refill your watering can early on.
- **Use paths** between fields so you can walk through without trampling.

## The animal area

- **Fence in your animals** with grass inside the pen so they can graze.
- **Keep the Coop and Barn near the Silo** for easy hay access.
- **Leave a grass patch** that regrows so you always have feed (animals eat grass before hay).

## Buildings and processing

- **Cluster your Kegs and Preserves Jars** in a Shed (or a shed-lined area) to save farm space.
- **Put the Silo in a corner** — you don't need to visit it often.
- **Keep a chest or two** near your fields for seeds and harvest overflow.

## Fruit trees

- **Fruit trees need the 8 surrounding tiles empty** to mature. Plant them in a dedicated orchard with space.
- Trees keep producing every season (their fruit season) once grown, so place them where they won't be in the way.

## Common layout mistakes

- **Blocking your own path** with buildings or untillable debris.
- **Planting too much to water by hand** before you have sprinklers.
- **No paths** — you'll accidentally walk over crops or grass.
- **Scattering buildings** — you'll waste time walking between them every day.

## The bottom line

You don't need a perfect farm — just **group things into zones, plan around sprinklers, and keep paths clear**. Start simple, and your farm will grow into an efficient, easy-to-run operation.
