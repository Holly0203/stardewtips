Energy is the real currency of Stardew Valley. Gold you can always earn more of, but a day spent face-down in a puddle of exhaustion is a day of watering, fishing, and friendship gone forever. The good news: running dry is almost entirely preventable. This guide explains exactly what the energy system does — and the most reliable ways to keep it near the top of the bar.

> **At a glance**
>
> - You start with **270 max energy**; Stardrops permanently add +34 each.
> - In bed **before midnight** = full energy next morning. Later than that and you lose part of it.
> - Hit zero and you're **exhausted**: no running, no fishing, and your next sleep only restores half your energy.
> - Pass out at **−15 energy** and the day ends — outdoors, that also costs 10% of your money (max 1,000g).
> - Free fixes: forage, ![Field Snack](/images/items/field-snack.png) Field Snacks, berry season gluttony, and the spa's full-service hot spring.

## What drains your energy

Almost every useful action costs energy. At level zero in each skill:

| Action | Energy cost |
|--------|-------------|
| Hoe / axe / pickaxe swing | 2 |
| Watering can (unupgraded) | 2 per tile |
| Watering can (Iridium, full charge) | 10 for up to 18 tiles |
| Fishing cast (any rod) | 8 |
| Throwing trash / swinging scythe | 0–2 |

Two things stand out. First, the **watering can is the biggest daily drain** in the early game — a 30-tile farm costs 60 energy every single morning. Second, **fishing is surprisingly expensive** at 8 energy per cast, which is why a full day of fishing with no snacks ends badly.

Upgraded tools don't cost less per swing — a [tool upgrade](/guides/tool-upgrades-guide/) makes each swing *cover more ground*, so the energy per tile tilled or watered drops sharply. An Iridium watering can waters 18 tiles for 10 energy; a basic one waters 18 tiles for 36.

## What happens when you hit zero

The energy bar can quietly go negative, and the game tells you about it in three escalating ways:

1. **Exhaustion (energy ≤ 0).** Your farmer moves slowly, can't run, and can't cast a rod. The status lasts until you sleep, and **sleep while exhausted restores only half your energy** the next morning.
2. **Passing out (energy ≤ −15).** The day ends immediately. If you collapse **outside your house you lose 10% of your held gold, up to 1,000g** — a brutal tax on greedy mining trips.
3. **The 2am curfew.** Still awake at 2:00am? You collapse wherever you are and take the same money penalty, even with a full energy bar.

The rule that follows: **never sleep in the exhausted state if you can help it.** Eating even one cheap foraged item to get back above zero before bed saves you half a day of energy.

## The best ways to restore energy

### 1. Sleep well (the free 90% of the answer)

Being in bed **before midnight** restores 100% of your energy. Every hour past midnight shaves the recovery down, and it stacks with the exhaustion penalty. A cheap clock and a habit of "home by 11:30pm" is worth more than any food strategy.

### 2. Eat cheap early: foraging and Field Snacks

Early Spring and Fall, the map is a free lunch. Spring Onions, Wild Horseradish, Dandelions, and leeks all restore a little energy and cost nothing but the walk.

Then there's the best early-game craft in the game: the ![Field Snack](/images/items/field-snack.png) **Field Snack** — one ![Acorn](/images/items/acorn.png) acorn + one ![Maple Seed](/images/items/maple-seed.png) maple seed + one ![Pine Cone](/images/items/pine-cone.png) pine cone, unlocked at Foraging level 1. It restores 45 energy and 18 health for literally zero gold. Seeds litter the floor under trees all year; craft 10–15 every weekend and you'll never black out in the mines again.

### 3. Feast during berry seasons

Twice a year, berry bushes go into overdrive:

- **Salmonberry: Spring 15–18** — ![Salmonberry](/images/items/salmonberry.png) bushes within the town area fruit all season.
- **Blackberry: Fall 8–11** — ![Blackberry](/images/items/blackberry.png) bushes near the quarry and forest pack your inventory.

Grab a few dozen berries during these windows and you carry a pocket full of 12–15-energy snacks for the rest of the season.

### 4. Cook real meals

Raw forage is a stopgap; cooked dishes are fuel. The first house upgrade (Robin at the Carpenter's Shop) adds a **kitchen**, and from that point dishes like Fried Egg, Omelet, Hashbrowns, and salad restore far more energy per slot than any single ingredient. ![Coffee](/images/items/coffee.png) Coffee is the outlier to remember: it restores only a little energy but gives a **speed boost** — perfect on Spring–Fall mornings when you're plant-heavy.

### 5. The spa: a free full restore

Down by the railroad, in the north-east of the map, the **spa's hot spring refills your energy and health for free**, restoring 10 points per second while you stand still in the pool. (In version 1.6 it's usable even in Spring; in older versions you had to wait for the Summer 3 earthquake to clear the railroad.) The trick veterans use: run your energy down fishing in the morning, dip at midday, and get a whole second day out of the same 24 hours.

## Habits that prevent the problem entirely

- **Build sprinklers.** They eliminate the largest daily energy drain — hand watering. Start with Basic Sprinklers in [the sprinkler guide](/guides/sprinkler-guide/).
- **Do heavy work first.** Clear stumps, till new fields, and hit the mines while your bar is full; save talking, shopping, and shipping bin runs for the fizzle.
- **Eat *before* you're empty.** Waiting for exhaustion means the half-recovery penalty looms over every remaining hour.
- **Carry a stack of Field Snacks everywhere** — mines, festivals, fishing marathons.
- **Use the shipping bin, not your feet.** Walking back to the house mid-day burns energy you could save by dumping at the field-edge chest.

## The bottom line

Energy management is 90% scheduling: **in bed by midnight, snacks in your pocket, watering automated, and the spa as your reset button.** Master those four habits and the exhaustion penalty becomes something that only happens to other people.
