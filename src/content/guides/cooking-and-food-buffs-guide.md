A grey recipe and a locked recipe mean different things. One needs ingredients; the other needs to be learned first. Once you can cook, separate the meal you take for its buff from the food you use to recover health during the trip.

This guide explains a small, useful kitchen setup and the buff rule most likely to matter during a fishing or mining trip.

> **At a glance**
>
> - The first farmhouse upgrade adds a kitchen and refrigerator.
> - Recipes must be learned as well as supplied with ingredients.
> - The stove uses items in your inventory, refrigerator, and connected Mini-Fridges.
> - One food buff and one drink buff can be active together.
> - Eating a different buff food replaces the active food buff; food without buffs does not.

## Decide whether the kitchen is the next useful purchase

Robin's first farmhouse upgrade costs **10,000g and 450 Wood**, according to the [Farmhouse reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Farmhouse).

Choose it when cooking serves a real goal rather than because you feel behind without a house expansion. If ordinary food supplies are already sufficient and several core farm projects remain unfunded, the kitchen can wait.

Conversely, if you are actively collecting recipes and planning regular buff meals, a kitchen supports an activity you are ready to use. Make room in the material budget for the upgrade rather than repeatedly spending the wood on unrelated small crafts.

## Learn recipes before hoarding every ingredient

The [Cooking reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Cooking) explains the difference between locked recipes and learned recipes with missing ingredients. Read the interface before assuming the problem is an empty fridge.

The television's [Queen of Sauce](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/The_Queen_of_Sauce) teaches Sunday recipes starting on Spring 7, with reruns on Wednesdays. Watching during the normal morning check is an easy habit even before you own a kitchen.

Other recipes come from different progression and relationship sources. Choose a few dishes you actually want to make, then identify what is missing: access, recipe knowledge, or ingredients. A focused list is easier to support than trying to stock the entire cooking collection at once.

## Keep valuable reserves out of the cooking supply

Ingredients in your inventory and cooking storage can be used by the interface. A crop or fish saved for a bundle should not sit in a general ingredient pool if a recipe could consume it.

Separate the non-negotiable reserve before cooking. This matters especially for recipes that accept a broad ingredient category rather than one exact item. Inspect what you are making and keep rare ingredients out of reach when an ordinary substitute will do.

Our [backpack and storage guide](/guides/backpack-storage-guide/) explains how to label temporary reserves. The refrigerator should contain things you are willing to cook, not every valuable edible item you own.

## Separate healing from the buff you want to preserve

Only one food's buffs are active at a time. Eating another buff food replaces the food effects already running. Food with no buffs can restore health or energy without replacing those effects. Drink buffs are tracked separately and can coexist with a food buff.

This creates a practical two-part food loadout: the meal chosen for its effect, plus a stack chosen for recovery. If you want to preserve a fishing-related food buff, do not automatically eat a different buff dish merely because it is next on the hotbar.

Check the item description before the trip. “Cooked food” is not a single category with identical behavior, and a dish that looks more elaborate is not necessarily the best recovery item for the outing.

## Four foods with different jobs

The table uses ordinary quality, no Qi Seasoning. Recovery is shown as **energy / health**; a restoration value is not a sale price.

| Food | Ingredients or accessible source | Recovery | Effect and intended job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salad | 220g at the Saloon; cooking uses Leek, Dandelion, Vinegar | 113 / 50 | No buff; portable recovery without a kitchen purchase |
| Cheese | Milk in a Cheese Press | 125 / 56 | Normal quality, no buff; recovery if the dairy already exists |
| Dish O' The Sea | Two Sardines + one Hashbrowns; recipe at Fishing 3 | 150 / 67 | +3 Fishing; use for the fishing window |
| Spicy Eel | Eel + Hot Pepper, or one Ruby at the Desert Trader | 115 / 51 | +1 Luck and +1 Speed; a travel/mining buff meal |

Sources: [Salad](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Salad), [Cheese](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Cheese), [Dish O' The Sea ingredients and effect](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Sardine#Recipes), and [Spicy Eel](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Spicy_Eel). Recipe ownership is still required when cooking. Buying or trading a finished meal does not teach its recipe.

For Dish O' The Sea, Hashbrowns is itself a recipe: one Potato plus one Oil, with a recipe sold by Gus for 50g. Save both Sardines before cooking so a different generic-fish recipe does not consume them. See [Hashbrowns](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Hashbrowns).

### Fishing example: buff meal plus recovery

Eat Dish O' The Sea before the intended session. Keep Salad or ordinary Cheese for restoration while preserving its Fishing buff. Eating Spicy Eel afterward would replace the food buff with Luck and Speed, removing the Fishing bonus. A separate compatible drink can coexist with the chosen food effect.

For a character using the +3 to meet a [legendary fish](/guides/legendary-fish-guide/) level threshold, that replacement changes eligibility as well as comfort. Check the active effect before another cast if a buff has expired.

### Mining example: buy a small recovery stack

Five Salads cost **1,100g** at the Saloon. That is a clear cash allowance for five recovery uses, not a guarantee of surviving a whole dungeon. Compare it with Cheese already produced at home before buying. Existing food has a sale opportunity cost, but buying a barn solely to supply one outing is a much larger project.

Start with the stack you can replenish and learn how quickly it is used. The [combat guide](/guides/combat-survival-guide/) explains accessible hotbar placement; food stored safely at home cannot restore health mid-fight.

## Cook in batches that match actual demand

Start with a few portions, use them on ordinary outings, then decide whether a larger reserve makes sense. If the meals remain untouched while ingredients become scarce, the cooking goal may be more ambitious than the routine it supports.

Do not assume cooking every ingredient improves its sale value. Choose dishes for their purpose, and compare any selling plan with the ingredients you are giving up. The kitchen is not automatically a replacement for your farm's existing processing strategy.

If your current problem is simply running out of stamina, our [energy guide](/guides/energy-management-tips/) can help reduce waste before you commit more resources to food production.
