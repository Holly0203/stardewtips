Use a gem forge when you want a predictable weapon-stat improvement. Use a Prismatic Shard enchantment when you are willing to accept a random eligible effect. They are separate systems, so you can improve a weapon without spending your only shard.

## Separate the material budgets

| Operation | Ingredient besides the weapon/tool | Cinder Shards |
|---|---|---:|
| First ordinary gem forge | One compatible gem | 10 |
| Second | One compatible gem | 15 |
| Third | One compatible gem | 20 |
| Enchantment | One Prismatic Shard | 20 |

Three chosen gem forges cost **45 shards**. Adding one enchantment brings the shard budget to **65**, plus three gems and a Prismatic Shard. Diamond is a special alternative: ten shards fill remaining forge slots with random upgrades, so it is not equivalent to selecting three effects yourself.

The [Forge reference](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Forge) documents the costs and effects. The station is at Volcano floor ten; unlock the [return shortcut](/guides/volcano-dungeon-first-forge-route/) so you can revisit without another full ascent.

## Choose a gem for the actual weapon problem

| Gem | Per-effect improvement | When to consider it |
|---|---|---|
| Ruby | +10% to minimum and maximum damage | Ordinary hits are too weak |
| Emerald | Weapon speed; repeated Emerald levels give +2, +3, +2 | Your chosen weapon feels too slow |
| Amethyst | +1 knockback | You want more separation after a hit |
| Topaz | +1 Defense | You prefer a defensive stat |
| Aquamarine | +4.6 percentage points critical chance | You are building around critical hits |
| Jade | +10% critical-hit power | Your build already benefits from critical hits |

For a first predictable change, consider one Ruby on a weapon you intend to keep. If a hypothetical weapon deals 40–60 damage before the forge, a ten-percent increase changes that range to 44–66 before other relevant calculations. This arithmetic does not predict damage against every enemy's defenses.

An Emerald serves a different purpose. If your trouble is finishing a slow swing before contact, try speed rather than assuming the highest damage number will feel best. Knockback can also make enemies harder to reach for follow-up swings; it is not an automatic improvement for every play style.

## Do not reroll by accident

An enchantment uses a Prismatic Shard and twenty shards each time. A new result replaces the existing enchantment instead of adding another ordinary enchantment slot. Tool upgrades retain an enchantment already applied.

Before another attempt, read the current effect and decide whether it already supports your work. For example, a tool property that reduces repeated labor can be useful even when a different online recommendation sounds more exciting. The next roll has a resource cost whether you like its result or not.

Keep the selected weapon or tool clear of unrelated inventory items when loading the interface. Check the preview and input before confirming. The goal is to improve the object you will use, not whichever object happened to be nearest the cursor.

## A practical first-visit choice

If you arrive with twenty shards and one Ruby, a first gem forge costs ten and leaves ten. You cannot also buy a twenty-shard enchantment from that remainder. Either make the predictable change now or keep the materials until another collection trip supports the other operation.

If combat is failing because healing is inaccessible, solve that before spending another gemstone. The [combat guide](/guides/combat-survival-guide/) and [food guide](/guides/cooking-and-food-buffs-guide/) address problems a weapon stat cannot fix.
